## Making Requests and Offers

Requests and offers help people coordinate. The language should make the action clear while respecting the listener's time, authority, and ability to say no.

### Scale the request

Small familiar requests can be direct. Larger, inconvenient, or formal requests usually need softer language.

- Small: "Can you send me the link?"
- More formal: "Could you send me the link when you have a moment?"
- Larger: "Would it be possible to review my draft before Thursday?"

Polite wording is useful, but the action and deadline must remain clear.

### Make offers specific

A specific offer is easier to accept than a general offer.

- General: "Let me know if you need anything."
- Specific: "Would you like me to review the first page?"
- Specific: "I can take notes during the meeting if that helps."

Specific offers show what help is available without taking control of the other person's work.

### Accept, decline, and negotiate

When accepting, confirm the important detail. When declining, give a brief reason if appropriate and suggest another option.

- "Yes, I can send it by 3 p.m."
- "I cannot finish it today, but I can send the first half tomorrow."
- "I am not available on Friday. Would Monday morning work?"

### Useful phrase bank

- "Could you possibly...?"
- "Would you mind...?"
- "Would you like me to...?"
- "I can help with..."
- "I am afraid I cannot..., but I can..."

> A polite request still needs a clear action, owner, and timing.

### Guided practice

Write one request to a friend, one to a lecturer, and one to a colleague. For each request, add an acceptance, a polite decline, and an alternative response.

### Key takeaway

Effective requests and offers balance clear action with language that fits the relationship and size of the favor.
