## Directions, Plans, and Schedules

Planning conversations combine locations, sequences, dates, times, and commitments. Clear reference points and confirmation questions prevent small misunderstandings from becoming missed meetings or wrong turns.

### Give directions from a shared point

Start from a location both people can identify. Use visible landmarks and ordered steps.

- "Start at the main gate."
- "Walk past the library."
- "Turn left after the coffee shop."
- "The classroom is across from the elevator."

For longer routes, divide the explanation into stages. Check that the listener recognizes the next major point before adding more detail.

### Make time clear

Use the day, date, time, and time zone when confusion is possible. Phrases such as "tomorrow" or "next Friday" can be ambiguous across countries, late-night messages, or busy schedules.

- "Tuesday, March 12, at 3 p.m."
- "Friday at 10 a.m. Vietnam time."
- "Does Wednesday afternoon work for you?"

### Confirm changes

When a plan changes, say what changed, what stayed the same, and whether the other person needs to act.

- "The place changed from Room 204 to Room 305. The time is still 2 p.m."
- "We are moving the meeting to Thursday. Please send your slides by Wednesday evening."

### Confirmation phrases

- "Just to confirm, we are meeting..."
- "So the final plan is..."
- "Have I got that right?"
- "Could you repeat the room number?"

> Plans become reliable when the final arrangement is repeated clearly.

### Guided practice

Explain a route from a bus stop to a classroom. Then arrange a study meeting with two possible times. Finish with one sentence that confirms the final plan.

### Key takeaway

Directions and schedules need shared reference points, ordered details, and explicit confirmation.
