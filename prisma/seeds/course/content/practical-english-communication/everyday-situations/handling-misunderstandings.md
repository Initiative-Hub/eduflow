## Handling Misunderstandings

Misunderstandings are normal, even between fluent speakers. Repair strategies help you pause, locate the unclear point, and rebuild shared meaning without blame.

### Identify the gap

Avoid saying only "I do not understand" when you can name the problem. A precise repair request helps the speaker respond.

- "Could you repeat the date?"
- "I missed the last instruction."
- "What does that word mean in this context?"
- "Do you mean the first file or the final file?"

### Choose a repair strategy

Different problems need different support.

1. Ask for repetition: "Could you say that again?"
2. Ask for slower speech: "Could you say it a little more slowly?"
3. Ask for spelling: "Could you spell the name?"
4. Ask for an example: "Can you give an example?"
5. Paraphrase: "So you want me to send the report today, correct?"

### Correct tactfully

When correcting yourself, take responsibility for the unclear message.

- "Sorry, I meant Thursday, not Tuesday."
- "I may not have explained that clearly."
- "Let me rephrase that."

When correcting another person, soften the interruption and give the accurate detail directly.

- "I think the meeting is at 10, not 9."
- "Just a small correction: the file is in the shared folder."

> Repair is a communication skill, not a failure.

### Guided practice

Role-play a conversation with an unclear deadline and an unfamiliar word. Use two repair strategies, then summarize the final meaning to confirm agreement.

### Key takeaway

Handle misunderstandings by identifying the unclear point, requesting the right support, and confirming the shared meaning.
