## Clear Emails and Messages

Effective messages respect the reader's attention. They make the purpose, required action, context, and timing easy to identify.

### Lead with purpose

The subject line should name the topic and action. The opening should explain why you are writing.

- Weak subject: "Question"
- Stronger subject: "Feedback request for presentation outline"

Weak opening: "I hope you are well. I wanted to ask something about the thing we discussed."

Stronger opening: "I am writing to ask for feedback on my presentation outline before Friday."

### Make action visible

Separate background from the request. Include exact dates, file names, links, or decisions.

```text
Could you review the attached outline by Thursday at 5 p.m.?

I am especially looking for feedback on:
- whether the main point is clear
- whether slide 3 has too much detail
- whether the conclusion needs a stronger next step
```

Bullets help when several parallel items must be scanned. A single simple request is often clearer as one sentence.

### Match tone to relationship

Professional tone does not require long apologies. Be courteous, concise, and specific.

- "Could you..." is polite and direct.
- "I apologize for disturbing you with this small question..." may be too heavy for a normal request.
- "Send it now" may sound too abrupt unless the relationship and urgency justify it.

### Closing clearly

End with the next step.

- "Thank you for your time."
- "Please let me know if Friday is not possible."
- "I will revise the outline after receiving your comments."

### Guided practice

Rewrite a long message requesting assignment feedback. Give it a specific subject line, place the request in the first two sentences, and include a clear deadline and attachment reference.

### Key takeaway

A clear message lets the reader understand its purpose and next action in one quick pass.
