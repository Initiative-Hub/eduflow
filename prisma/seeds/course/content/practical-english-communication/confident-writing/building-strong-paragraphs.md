## Building Strong Paragraphs

A paragraph develops one main idea through connected sentences. Readers understand it quickly when the topic is clear, support is relevant, and each sentence moves the idea forward.

### Control the main idea

A topic sentence names the subject and makes a specific point. It should not be so broad that the paragraph can go anywhere.

Weak: "Exercise is important."

Stronger: "Short daily walks can improve student concentration during long study periods."

The stronger sentence gives the paragraph a clear direction.

### Develop, do not repeat

Supporting sentences should explain, prove, illustrate, compare, or qualify the main idea. Repeating the topic sentence with different words creates length without development.

Useful support can include:

- A reason.
- An example.
- A short explanation.
- A contrast.
- A result.

### Create cohesion

Transitions show relationships between ideas. Repeated key terms and clear pronouns help readers follow the subject.

- Addition: "also", "in addition"
- Contrast: "however", "on the other hand"
- Example: "for example", "for instance"
- Result: "therefore", "as a result"

Use connectors only when the relationship is real.

### Paragraph review checklist

- [ ] The paragraph has one main idea.
- [ ] The topic sentence is specific.
- [ ] Every supporting sentence develops the topic.
- [ ] Transitions show real relationships.
- [ ] The final sentence closes or prepares the next idea.

### Guided practice

Write a paragraph arguing for one improvement to your study routine. Underline the topic sentence, label each support sentence, and remove anything that does not develop the central idea.

### Key takeaway

A strong paragraph makes one focused claim and develops it with relevant, connected support.
