## Active Listening and Follow-ups

Strong communication is not only speaking fluently. Active listeners show attention, identify the main point, and respond in ways that help the speaker continue or clarify.

### Listen for structure

Do not try to remember every word. Listen for signals that organize ideas.

- "First" introduces a sequence.
- "However" shows contrast.
- "The main reason" points to a key idea.
- "As a result" connects cause and effect.

Focus on the speaker's purpose, key nouns, and changes in direction.

### Show attention naturally

Short response signals show that you are following without taking over.

- "I see."
- "Right."
- "That makes sense."
- "Really?"
- "Go on."

Use them sparingly. If every sentence receives the same response, the signal begins to sound automatic.

### Paraphrase to confirm

Paraphrasing turns listening into visible understanding.

- "So you mean the deadline changed, but the task stayed the same?"
- "If I understood correctly, your main concern is the budget."
- "You are saying the first option is faster, but less reliable."

### Ask follow-up questions

A good follow-up connects to what the speaker just said.

- "What happened after that?"
- "Why was that the most important issue?"
- "Can you give an example?"
- "How did you decide?"

> A follow-up question proves you listened because it grows from the speaker's idea.

### Guided practice

Listen to a two-minute interview or explanation. Write the main point, two supporting details, one paraphrase, and one follow-up question. Then compare your notes with a partner.

### Key takeaway

Active listening turns attention into response signals, paraphrases, and questions that build shared understanding.
