## Introducing Yourself

A good introduction is short, relevant, and easy to answer. It gives the listener enough information to place you in the situation, then opens a path for the conversation to continue.

### Choose details for the situation

Start with your name. Add one or two details that match the setting: your role, field of study, hometown, goal, or reason for attending. Do not list everything about yourself at once.

- In class: "Hi, I am Linh. I am studying web development, and I am interested in accessible design."
- At work: "Hello, I am Minh. I joined the product team this month and will help with user research."
- At an event: "Hi, I am An. I came because I am exploring frontend roles."

### Adjust formality

Formal introductions often use complete phrases and role-related details. Casual introductions can use contractions, simpler phrasing, and personal interests. In both cases, speak clearly and avoid memorized language that does not fit the moment.

### Keep the exchange alive

End with a question or shared observation. This gives the other person an easy next turn.

- "How did you hear about this event?"
- "What project are you working on?"
- "Have you taken a class with this teacher before?"
- "What brought you to this team?"

> A useful introduction is not a speech. It is the first turn in a conversation.

### Practice steps

1. Write a 20-second introduction for a class.
2. Write a 20-second introduction for a job interview.
3. Remove any detail that does not help the listener respond.
4. Record both versions and listen for pace and clarity.

### Self-check

- [ ] I included my name.
- [ ] I chose details that fit the setting.
- [ ] I avoided giving too many facts.
- [ ] I ended with a natural opening for the other person.

### Guided practice

Role-play meeting a new classmate, a project teammate, and an interviewer. Use the same basic identity, but change the details and formality for each listener.

### Key takeaway

An effective introduction gives relevant information and invites the other person to continue naturally.
