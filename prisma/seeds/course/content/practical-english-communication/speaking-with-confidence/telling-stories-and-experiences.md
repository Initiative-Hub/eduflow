## Telling Stories and Experiences

Short stories make conversations memorable. They help you explain experiences, lessons, problems, and opinions. A listener needs context, sequence, and a reason the story matters.

### Set the scene efficiently

Begin with only the background the listener needs.

- When did it happen?
- Where were you?
- Who was involved?
- What normal situation was about to change?

Avoid long introductions that delay the main event.

### Move through events clearly

Use time markers and consistent tense. Highlight the turning point instead of giving equal time to every small action.

- "At first..."
- "Then..."
- "Suddenly..."
- "Meanwhile..."
- "Eventually..."
- "In the end..."

### Make the ending meaningful

A story should end with a result, reaction, or reflection.

- Result: "We finished the project one day early."
- Reaction: "I felt nervous, but also proud."
- Reflection: "I learned to ask for help earlier."

Without an ending, the listener may wonder why the story was told.

### Story frame

1. Setting: where and when.
2. Normal situation: what was expected.
3. Turning point: what changed.
4. Result: what happened.
5. Reflection: why it matters.

> A good story does not include every detail. It includes the details that serve the point.

### Guided practice

Plan a 90-second story about a small challenge using five notes: setting, normal situation, turning point, result, and reflection. Tell it without reading full sentences.

### Key takeaway

A compelling short story guides the listener from context through a turning point to a meaningful result.
