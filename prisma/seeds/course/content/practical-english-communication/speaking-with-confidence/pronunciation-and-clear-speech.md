## Pronunciation and Clear Speech

Clear speech is not about removing an accent. It is about helping listeners identify important words, follow the rhythm of the message, and understand contrasts that affect meaning.

### Stress carries meaning

English words usually have one stressed syllable. Sentences also stress content words, new information, or contrast.

- "I ordered the BLUE notebook." The color is important.
- "I ORDERED the blue notebook." The action is important.
- "I ordered the blue NOTEBOOK." The item is important.

Changing stress changes what the listener hears as new or important.

### Practice contrasts in context

Some sound differences affect intelligibility more than others. Practice them in short words, then in phrases.

- ship / sheep
- live / leave
- rice / lice
- fan / van

Do not practice isolated sounds forever. Move quickly into useful phrases and sentences.

### Use thought groups

Pause between meaningful chunks, not after every word. Thought groups make long sentences easier to follow.

```text
After the meeting / I will send the revised file / and you can check the final section.
```

A steady pace with clear grouping often sounds more confident than speaking quickly.

### Recording routine

1. Mark stressed words.
2. Add slashes for thought groups.
3. Record once at a natural speed.
4. Listen for unclear words.
5. Record again with one improvement.

> Clear pronunciation prioritizes meaning, rhythm, and listener understanding.

### Guided practice

Choose a short paragraph. Mark sentence stress and thought groups. Record it twice and compare clarity. Then practice one difficult sound contrast in five useful phrases.

### Key takeaway

Clear speech comes from meaningful stress, useful sound contrasts, and organized pacing.
