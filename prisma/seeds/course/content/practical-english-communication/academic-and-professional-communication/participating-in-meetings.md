## Participating in Meetings

Meeting participation involves entering the discussion, contributing a relevant point, responding to others, and confirming decisions. Useful language helps you contribute without dominating or disappearing.

### Join at the right moment

Connect your contribution to the current topic.

- "Could I add something here?"
- "That connects to the issue we saw yesterday."
- "Before we move on, I have one question about the timeline."

Beginning with relevance helps others understand why you are taking the floor.

### Make contributions actionable

A useful update includes status, evidence, blockers, and the decision or support needed.

```text
Status: The first draft is complete.
Evidence: Two users tested it yesterday.
Blocker: We still need the final images.
Need: Can design send them by Wednesday?
```

When raising a concern, pair it with a question or next step.

### Respond to others

Show that you heard the previous point before adding your own.

- "I agree with the timeline concern."
- "I see the benefit, but I am worried about the cost."
- "Could you clarify who owns that action?"

### Close the loop

Before changing topics or ending, summarize decisions, owners, and deadlines.

- "So Minh will update the slides by Tuesday."
- "I will send the notes after this meeting."
- "Have I captured that correctly?"

### Guided practice

Simulate a project meeting with an update, a disagreement, and a decision. Practice entering once, asking one clarifying question, and giving a final action summary.

### Key takeaway

Effective meeting language connects contributions to the discussion and turns decisions into clearly owned actions.
